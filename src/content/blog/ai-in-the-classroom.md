I use AI tools every day. At home for small personal projects, at work for lesson planning, feedback drafts, and the occasional explanation I need to rephrase for a class that has not quite got it yet.

This is not a manifesto about AI transforming education. I am a bit tired of those. It is more a set of practical observations after about a year of doing this in an actual classroom with actual students.

## What it is actually useful for

**Drafting feedback.** Writing individual feedback on thirty submissions is slow and, after the fifteenth student, tends to get repetitive. AI helps me draft a version I can then adjust. The adjusting still takes time, but I am starting from something rather than a blank page.

**Rephrasing explanations.** When I have explained something twice and it has not landed, I sometimes ask an AI to explain the same concept using a different metaphor or approach. It does not always work, but it generates options I would not have thought of immediately.

**Planning.** "Here is the topic, here are the constraints, here is what they already know — suggest a structure." Useful as a starting point. I always change it.

## What it is not useful for

Replacing the part where I actually know the students. The AI does not know that this particular student works better with concrete examples before abstractions, or that this group needs five minutes of something lighter before they can focus. That part is still mine.

It is also not useful when I do not know enough about the topic myself to evaluate what it says. That happens more than I would like to admit.

## What the students make of it

They already use it. Widely. Most of them are not particularly reflective about *how* — they use it to get things done. Teaching them to think about when it helps and when it misleads them is, I think, now part of the job.

---

*These are observations, not conclusions. Things will probably look different in a year.*
