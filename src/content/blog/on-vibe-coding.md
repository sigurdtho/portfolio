There is a term floating around now: *vibe coding*. As far as I can tell, it means writing software mostly by describing what you want and letting an AI figure out the implementation. You guide, you steer, you fix things when they go wrong, but you are not typing every line yourself.

I have been doing a version of this for the past year or so. I want to write down what it actually feels like from where I sit — someone with a background in informatics who has not worked as a developer for a while and whose main job is teaching.

## It is slower than it looks in demos

The demo version: you describe something, the AI produces it, it works.

The reality: you describe something, the AI produces *a version* of it that almost works. You fix one thing and something else breaks. You ask it to fix that, and it introduces a new problem. After three or four rounds you have something that works, and you understand it better than you would have if you had just copied the final version from somewhere.

This is not a complaint. The back-and-forth is, in a strange way, a decent way to learn.

## You still need to know enough to steer

The limiting factor is not the AI's ability to generate code. It is my ability to know when the generated code is reasonable. If I do not have enough of a mental model of what I am building, I end up accepting things that are subtly wrong, and I do not notice until later.

This has pushed me to read more carefully about the things I am building, not less.

## What you can actually build this way

Small things that solve specific problems. A tool that tracks something you want to track. A small website. An automation that handles a task that was annoying you.

The useful scope is limited, but within that scope it works well. And the threshold for "worth building" has dropped considerably. Things that would previously have taken me two weekends now take an evening. That changes what I bother to start.

---

*I am not going to tell you this is the future of software development. I have no idea. I am just saying it is a reasonable way to build small things if you have some background and a problem worth solving.*
